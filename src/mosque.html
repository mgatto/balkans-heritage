The Mosque

From the hills, rises a mosque…

Built in 1531, the Gazi Husrev-beg Mosque stands as the most important Islamic architectural monument in Bosnia and Herzegovina. This magnificent structure represents the pinnacle of Ottoman architecture in the Balkans.

Located in the heart of Baščaršija, Sarajevo's old bazaar quarter, the mosque was commissioned by Gazi Husrev-beg, the Ottoman governor of Bosnia from 1521 to 1541. The mosque complex includes a madrasa (Islamic school), a library, a clock tower, and a hammam (public bath), making it a complete Islamic cultural center.

The mosque's architectural significance lies in its perfect proportions and the innovative use of space. Its central dome, supported by four massive pillars, creates an impressive interior space that can accommodate up to 1,000 worshippers. The mosque's courtyard, with its elegant fountain and surrounding arcades, provides a peaceful retreat from the bustling streets of Baščaršija. Despite suffering damage during the 1992-1995 siege of Sarajevo, the mosque has been meticulously restored to its former glory, standing as a testament to Bosnia's rich Islamic heritage and architectural prowess.

— UNESCO World Heritage
